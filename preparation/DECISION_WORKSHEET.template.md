# Decision worksheet — Scientific Writing with AI

**GOAL:** jointly define the context that will guide AI chats or the scientific repository's agent, according to the collaborators' agreed level of involvement. **Initial state: the preliminary discussion has not yet taken place; no agreement is assumed.**

Complete a private copy. `[to complete]` is an empty field; `?` is an open decision. Human1 and Human2 are placeholders to replace or extend. No checkbox is preselected. Accept human prompts in any language and preserve the substance of answers in translation. Write saved files in English by default unless the human explicitly requests another language for specified files or passages; the prompt language alone does not change file language. README files stay English unless the explicit request includes them.

**Two distinct roles:** the toolkit's policy-building AI works with broad autonomy within its user's environment and governing instructions. It may research, draft, translate, organise and test policy proposals as the task requires. The decisions below define the future scientific agent's permissions: unanswered questions do not suspend the builder's already authorised preparatory work, and a proposed answer does not become a shared agreement without human confirmation.

## 3. Activity catalogue

**P:** permitted within its recorded scope with ordinary human review as specified in the decision register. **C:** permitted only under explicit conditions. **V:** excluded from the project. **?:** undecided; the scientific agent must not perform it and must ask when relevant. **NA:** not applicable, not a permission.

For P, record the responsible human and applicable ordinary review procedure in the conditions/checks column; preserve them in the generated decision register. For C, specify at least the intervention boundary, materials, human checks, responsible person and output limits. Until those conditions are defined, the scientific activity cannot be enabled. For group decisions, list the IDs and exceptions. These statuses govern the future scientific workflow, not the builder's preparation of policy options.

L0 = mechanical intervention; L1 = writing; L2 = scientific checking; L3 = research contribution. These are descriptions, not permission levels. Distinguish faithful transcription, correction, interpretation and generation; even a proposal in a note can exceed a scientific boundary.

### Examples to adopt, change or reject

- Possible colours: human content in black; AI correction in red; AI proposal in orange. In note mode: margin notes with a light-red/orange background. No colour authorises a prohibited or unresolved activity.
- Possible bibliography audit: every entry, including uncited ones; find URLs/DOIs, open records, compare metadata and versions, update only verified matches, retain warnings for uncertainty. Specify the backend and warning syntax first; a DOI may legitimately be unavailable.
- Possible record: `ID | Recorded on | Human | Tool | Models | Activity / affected material | Evidence / output status | Checks / unknowns | First effect commit`.
- Possible header: a `%+---+` box, lines `%| NOTE`, `%| DATE created`, `%| DATE modified`, `%| Author`, `%| Credits`; `%- D0cum3nt`, `%- Packages`, `%- T1tle` separators for standalone documents. Do not add a preamble to included sections.
- For its own maintenance, the toolkit uses `[AI]` for approximately 90% generated content and `[AI-Human]` for collaborative development. Reconcile this with the project's actual instructions: it does not certify scientific authorship or authorise Git operations. An absent tag does not prove absence of AI in an unverified history.
